Readiness Checklist

Thirty minutes, five questions, one paper trail

You will be asked these questions this week — by your board, your biggest customer, or your own team. Each one is answerable with evidence. Here is the evidence to collect, in order.

Start with the free assessment
The checklist

What to confirm, and what proves it

"Do we run PHP anywhere on the public internet?"

Prove it with: a full list of public hosts and their detected stacks. Admin panels, legacy apps and forgotten subdomains are where unpatched PHP hides. PHP2Shell briefing →

Sentinel check: host discovery + service fingerprinting on your apex.

"Are we on the fixed PHP versions?"

Prove it with: version per host, patched date, owner. Fixed releases are 8.5.12, 8.4.27, 8.3.36 and 8.2.35 — with backports for end-of-life branches. Details →

Sentinel check: version exposure reported per host where publicly observable.

"Do any of our certificates chain through the affected CA?"

Prove it with: issuer and chain for every public endpoint, cross-referenced against the revoked intermediates. eMudhra briefing →

Sentinel check: TLS and chain analysis on every live host in the scan.

"What actually is exposed to the outside?"

Prove it with: a dated external assessment — not an internal inventory. The outside view includes the staging box nobody remembered and the DNS record added last Tuesday.

Sentinel check: the full risk score, with findings ranked by business impact.

"What is the plan, and who owns it?"

Prove it with: a prioritized remediation list with owners and dates, plus a re-scan date to confirm closure. A plan without a verification step is a wish.

Sentinel check: remediation appendix in the PDF, then re-scan to move the score.

One sentence that survives the meeting: "We assessed our external surface on 23 October, during the active advisory window; findings and owners are attached; we re-scan on the date shown." Everything above exists to make that sentence true.
During a crisis window

How to communicate without inflating

Do

Lead with dates and sources. State what you verified and what you have not yet checked. Separate the advisories — they are different problems. Give a next checkpoint with a time attached.

Don't

Merge unrelated incidents into one story. Claim mass exploitation without a source and a timestamp. Say a system is affected before you have looked at it. Overstate today, and tomorrow's correction becomes the story.

This guidance mirrors the discipline we hold our own reports to: every claim carries an observation, a source, and a date — or it does not ship.
