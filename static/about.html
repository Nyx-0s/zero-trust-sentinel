Company

We think most companies are one honest measurement from being fine

Security spending rises every year, and the question boards ask most — are we exposed? — still tends to be answered with a feeling. We built Sentinel to replace the feeling with a date-stamped observation.

Not to sell fear. To remove it.

You are probably fine. That is exactly the problem. So the company has a second half: Mischief — scoped adversarial engagements that test that assumption instead of trusting it.

Principles

The standard we hold ourselves to

🔍

Evidence beats claims

Every finding names the observation that produced it. If we did not see it on your public surface, it does not appear in your report — enforced by a Critic pass that exists to cut unsupported statements.

⚖

Restraint is a feature

Public data only. No exploitation, no intrusive testing, no login attempts. It keeps assessments safe to share with customers and auditors, and it keeps us inside the line every responsible scanner respects. Offensive engagements run under a different, explicitly authorized rule set — the operating rules below.

📈

Findings come with fixes

A vulnerability list without a next action is a liability with extra steps. Every report carries prioritized, paste-ready remediation and a way to prove it worked.

🛡

Plain language, business stakes

Executives should not need a decoder ring. Each risk is written as impact: what it costs, who it affects, why it matters this quarter.

Why now

The outside view changed faster than the inside view

Attack tooling has become cheap enough that a lone operator can run what used to require a team, and this month's disclosures — a critical PHP pair, a certificate authority compromise, AI-agent-driven intrusions at named enterprises — arrived within seventy-two hours of one another.

Most internal security programs were built to watch what the organization knows about: the assets on the inventory, the servers with agents. The perimeter does not care about the inventory. Subdomains appear, certificates rotate, staging environments leak into public DNS on a Tuesday afternoon.

Zero-Trust Sentinel watches the side of you that strangers already see — continuously, in minutes rather than quarters, with output a board can read and an engineer can act on.

Operating rules

The constitution the crew runs under

Chaos is only useful if it stays inside lines drawn in advance. Four rules govern every Mischief engagement — written before, not during.

Scope is authorized once

One-time human act: you approve the authorized targets, the blast radius, and the emergency stop. Everything after that happens inside those boundaries — autonomously, on the record.

The hard boundary

No action outside authorized scope, without an engagement, or without an evidence record. If scope cannot be confirmed mid-run, the run stops and asks — it never guesses.

Stop first, report second

Scope exceeded, target deauthorized, real incident risk, or your emergency stop: the run halts immediately. The report then explains exactly what stopped it and where it stood.

Humans touch the exception queue

The engine handles the routine; people handle only the exceptions — a boundary question, an incident signal, a decision that needs a human signature.

What we will not do

Lines we do not cross

No inflated threats

We do not claim mass exploitation, coordinated campaigns, or that you are under attack without evidence specific to that claim. Fear that a prospect can disprove costs us the relationship.

No intrusive scanning

We do not exploit, brute-force or log in to anything. Your assessment uses only what any member of the public could see.

No invented findings

Every claim in every report traces to an observation on your surface. Unverifiable statements are removed before you see them.

Judge us by the first report

The free assessment is the whole product, up front. If the evidence is not there, you will know in two minutes.
