The payroll

Five contractors. All of them do their job. None of them are permanent.

Mischief runs on a payroll of autonomous AI contractors with testing specialties. They were named after Decepticons because the brand should at least be honest about the energy in the room.

They are hired per run, paid per invoice, and replaced the moment they stop earning it. The brand is the joke. The product is the proof.

The contractors

The testing payroll

Five specialties, five contractors, one invoice. Every one of them leaves written findings with evidence attached.

Functional end-to-end

Megatron

Walks the functional journey and checks whether the public promise matches the reconciled surface — identity, flow, interactive regions.

Front-end

Starscream

Tears at the visible layer: metadata, presentation, semantics, and interactive controls. Dark theme is fine; color-only meaning is not.

Back-end

Shockwave

Reads what the public edge betrays — hosting, caching, hidden services — and never mistakes a polished page for a proven absence.

Unit

Soundwave

Takes the small claims apart: are they testable, consistent, computed? Or cosmetic numbers on a demo state?

Integrated end-to-end

Optimus

Tests the whole thing as one chain: does the experience behave as a real system, or as isolated costume pieces?

The hiring model

Freelancers, paid per invoice

Each contractor is interviewed, takes the job, and is paid when the work ships. No drama, no offboarding, no references to fake.

The operating crew

Who keeps the run inside the lines

The contractors do the tearing. The crew makes sure every action stays scoped, logged, and stoppable — the one-time human act is your signature on the scope, nothing after it.

Prodigy

The production chief. Runs the machinery that finds the paths and decides which trails are worth following.

Magnifier

The pattern mind. Turns raw findings into behaviour, trends, and the narrative a board can act on.

Ringmaster

The pressure observer. Watches the show while it runs and keeps its performance honest.

Jester

The provocation writer. Writes pitches and probes, and asks the uncomfortable question nobody scheduled time for.

Ledger

The boundary keeper. Logs every action. If scope cannot be confirmed, the run stops and asks the Ledger. If the Ledger does not remember it, it did not happen properly.

The customer message rule

Never a meaningless message. Never a promise we cannot keep. Every message to a customer must either carry a finding or carry a boundary.

What you receive

Mischief does not sell comfort. It sells evidence.

Every engagement ends with the same company deliverable — not a joke with footnotes.

Surface summary

What was inspected, when, from where, and the exact status it returned.

Contractor findings

Each finding with its severity and its evidence line — every claim tied to what was observed.

Honest caveats

What was inferred, what could not be proven from the outside, and where the boundary of the run sat.

Invoice with line items

One line per contractor, one total, no mystery. Scope, findings, price — in that order.
