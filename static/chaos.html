The work

Five contractors, one chain

The burn is not a vulnerability scan wearing a costume. Each contractor tears at one layer of the promise your public surface makes, then the chain is tested as a whole.

Functional end-to-end

Megatron walks the journey

Checks whether the public promise matches the reconciled surface: identity, flow, and interactive-region risk.

Front-end

Starscream tears at the visible layer

Metadata, presentation, semantics, and interactive controls — including whether meaning survives without color.

Back-end

Shockwave reads the edge

Hosting, caching, and hidden services the public response betrays, plus the caveat a polished page proves nothing about what is behind it.

Unit

Soundwave takes claims apart

Every numeric claim tested in isolation: is it computed, static, or cosmetic? Testable, consistent, not theater.

Integrated end-to-end

Optimus runs the whole chain

The decisive question: is the experience a working system or a brochure? Verified through interaction, not structure.

Deliverable

Reports, not theater

Every run leaves written findings with severities, an evidence trail for each one, and an invoice with line items. Mischief does not sell comfort. It sells evidence.

How it starts

Three things, then the crew takes over

One-time human act: you authorize the scope. After that, the run happens inside it — autonomously, with a stop condition you control.

Authorized targets

The exact domains, environments, and accounts the crew may touch. Nothing outside the list, ever.

Blast radius

How far a chain may go before it stops: what it may touch, what it must only demonstrate, and nothing beyond.

Emergency stop

A customer-controlled kill switch. If scope cannot be confirmed mid-run, the crew stops and asks first.

What is not up for discussion: no destructive testing, no account takeover attempts, no flooding, no credential guessing, no exfiltration — and no scope without boundaries. Stop first, report second.
